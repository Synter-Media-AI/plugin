# Outbound Research

Find a checkable fact about the person, or do not mail them.

## The test

For one real lead on the list, ask: **if they replied "where did you get my information?", what would I say?**

If the honest answer is "you match an industry and a job title," the research is not done. Cut the cohort rather than send it. That question is not hypothetical — it is a verbatim reply from a real campaign that mailed 750 people a character-identical email.

## 1. Disqualifiers first — they veto, they do not score

Check these before any positive research. One hit stops the send, no matter how strong the positives.

| Check | Blocks because |
|---|---|
| In the CRM already | known contact or open deal |
| We have already met | cold copy insults someone whose call you attended |
| Current paying customer | they already buy from us |
| Contacted before, any channel | double-touch reads as spam |
| Suppressed, bounced, unsubscribed | legal and deliverability |

A **lapsed** customer is the deliberate exception: a win-back target, and a strong one. Say so plainly rather than pretending it is a cold approach.

## 2. Positive signals — use the strongest you can actually fetch

One fact per email. Never stack them.

```
they clicked our ad ............... strongest    30 day window
they used the product ............ very strong   45 day window
they engaged our social post ..... strong        60 day window  (comment > reaction)
someone at their company visited . moderate      30 day window  (company-level only)
new role, promotion, funding ..... strong        90 day window
role + industry only ............. NOT A SIGNAL — do not send
```

Ranking rules:

1. **Recency beats strength within a tier.** A reaction from yesterday beats an ad click from four weeks ago.
2. **Honour the decay windows.** Opening with "I saw you engaged with my post" about something from March is worse than no personalization.
3. **Person-level beats company-level.** "You clicked" is a fact. "Someone at your company visited" is an inference and must be written as one.

## 3. Land it without the AI tell

**Never name the surveillance.** The fact goes in, the mechanism stays out.

```
BAD   "I noticed you visited our pricing page on Tuesday."
BAD   "Our system flagged that you clicked one of our ads."
GOOD  "Your take on the PMax thread stuck with me."
GOOD  "You were looking at programmatic pricing recently, so this may be timely."
```

The test: would the sentence be creepy said aloud at a conference? If yes, reference the topic, not the event.

**Banned openers** — the tells that read as generated:

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I noticed that..." / "I saw that you..." / "I came across your profile"
- "As a {TITLE} at {COMPANY}, you probably..."
- Em-dashes anywhere
- The word "click" — Synter is prompt-driven; say "one prompt"

**Shape of the opening:**

```
line 1   timing or season anchor, short and human
line 2   the fact, stated as theirs not ours
line 3   why it matters to them specifically
```

Keep the fact out of line 1. Leading with it is what makes it feel like a merge field. A human beat first, then specificity, is what makes it feel written. Body under 90 words.

## 4. Timing anchors

| Send day | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | "kicking off the week" | **"Happy Monday!"** — the most-used cold-email opener there is; it signals bulk mail instantly |
| Tue-Wed | nothing, these are the best days | forcing an anchor |
| Thu | "before this week gets away" | |
| Fri | "Before the weekend," | asking for a call today |
| Sat-Sun | do not send cold | |

Rotate anchors across a cohort — the same one on every email is its own tell. And only use a day anchor if the schedule actually matches it; "before the weekend" landing on Tuesday is worse than nothing.

## 5. Seasonal and fiscal

Read from **their** calendar, not ours. For a DTC brand in October the anchor is peak retail season, not our quarter.

- **Jan** new budget, planning reopens · **Feb-Mar** Q1 under pressure
- **Apr-Jun** mid-year reforecast · **Jul-Aug** thin teams, keep asks small
- **Sep** everyone back, strongest window of the year
- **Oct-Nov** Q4 planning, next-year budget locking, retail peak
- **Dec** do not start sequences after the second week

## 6. Life events — the strongest human hook

| Event | Window | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| New role | **first 90 days**, the best window there is | choosing a stack, wants early wins |
| Promotion | 60 days | scope grew, budget grew |
| Funding | 90 days | pressure to deploy into growth |
| Team growth | 60 days | hiring paid roles means scaling spend |

Congratulate in **one clause**, then get to the substance. A paragraph about their promotion is transparently a device.

```
GOOD  "Three weeks into the growth seat - you're probably still
       picking what the paid stack looks like."
BAD   "Congratulations on your well-deserved promotion! It's always
       exciting to see talented leaders like yourself step into..."
```
